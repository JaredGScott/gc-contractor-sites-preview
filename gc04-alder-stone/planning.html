Older-home guide

Before You Renovate an Older Home

A house built in the 1920s rewards a careful look before anyone draws a new plan. These are the four things we look at first.

01

Walk the whole house

We look at every room, the attic and the basement: the structure, earlier remodels, original materials and how light comes into each room. Bring any old plans, surveys or inspection reports.

02

Make a keep list

Together we list what makes the house yours: trim, doors, floors, windows, built-ins, the stair. Some things stay in place, some get repaired, and some come out carefully and go back in.

03

Check what’s behind the walls

Knob-and-tube wiring, old galvanized pipes, missing insulation and tired framing are common in houses this age. We tell you what we know, what we suspect, and what needs a closer look.

04

Plan how old meets new

We work out floor heights, trim profiles and materials so the new rooms sit comfortably next to the old ones.

Common questions

Questions people ask about older homes

Can you match our original trim and floors?

Usually. We can have trim milled to match, and patch heart pine or oak floors with reclaimed boards. When an exact match isn’t possible, we’ll show you the closest options.

Is it worth saving our old windows?

Often, yes. Old wood windows can usually be repaired and weatherstripped. We’ll look at each one and tell you honestly which are worth saving.

What surprises should we expect in an older house?

Old wiring, galvanized plumbing, uneven framing and missing insulation are the common ones. We include an allowance for them in the proposal and walk you through anything we find.

Do you handle historic district approvals?

If your house is in one of the city’s Old and Historic Districts, exterior work needs review first. We help prepare what’s needed and plan the schedule around it.

What if the scope changes?

We write up a change order with the cost and any effect on the schedule, and nothing changes until you sign it.
