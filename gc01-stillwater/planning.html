Our process

How a Stillwater Custom Home Comes Together

Building on your own land takes more planning than most people expect. Here’s how we take it one step at a time, and what you’ll need at each step.

01

Walk the land

We meet you on the property and look at access, slope, trees, drainage, views, and where power, water and septic will come from. If the lot needs a survey or a soils report, we’ll tell you before you spend money on design.

02

Design the house

You work with your architect, or one we recommend, on a plan that fits the site. We price the design as it develops, so you can see what each big decision costs while it’s still easy to change.

03

Price it and put it in writing

When the drawings are done, you get a detailed proposal: every line item, an allowance for each thing you’ll pick out later, and a list of what’s not included. You’ll know who does what before you sign.

04

Permits and breaking ground

We submit for permits, order long-lead items like windows, and set the schedule. Once construction starts you’ll have one project manager, a written update every week, and regular walkthroughs as the house goes up.

Common questions

Questions people ask before they build

Do I need to own land before I call you?

No. We’re happy to look at a lot with you before you buy it. A walk-through can tell you a lot about what it will take to build there.

Do you design the homes too?

We work alongside architects and designers rather than drawing houses ourselves. If you don’t have one yet, we can introduce you to a few we’ve built with.

How much does it cost to build a custom home here?

It depends on the site, the size and the finishes. Site work alone, like clearing, septic and a long driveway, can vary a lot from one lot to the next. We’ll give you a realistic range once we’ve seen the property and your plans.

Who handles the permits?

We do. We submit the building permit, coordinate septic and site approvals with your engineer, and schedule the inspections.

What if we change something during construction?

We write up a change order with the cost and any effect on the schedule, and nothing changes until you sign it.
