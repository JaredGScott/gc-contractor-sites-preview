Before you start

What to Have Ready Before We Come Out

You don’t need plans or a budget figured out. These four things make the first visit a lot more useful.

01

What’s bugging you

Tell us what doesn’t work today and what you’d change if you could. “The kitchen is too small” is a fine place to start.

02

What you already have

Photos, old drawings, a survey, the inspection report from when you bought the house. Whatever you’ve got helps.

03

How you live

Kids, pets, working from home, the one bathroom you can’t lose. We plan the work around it.

04

What you want to spend

A rough range is enough. We’ll tell you what fits in it and what might need to wait.

Common questions

Questions homeowners ask us

Do you do the work yourselves or sub it out?

Some of both. Our own carpenters run the job and do the carpentry. Electrical, plumbing and HVAC are usually subcontractors we’ve worked with for years. We’ll tell you exactly who is doing what on your job.

Do I need drawings before I call?

No. Photos and a description are enough to start. If your job needs drawings for the permit, we’ll tell you what kind and who can do them.

How do you come up with the price?

We look at the house, define the scope and price the materials and labor. The first visit gives you a rough idea; the written proposal has the detailed price and lists anything we’re still unsure about.

Who pulls the permits?

Usually we do, and we schedule the inspections. Some jobs need an architect or engineer to prepare drawings first. We’ll sort that out before you sign anything.

What if we change our minds halfway through?

It happens. We write up the change, what it costs and what it does to the schedule, and you sign off before we do it.
