About us

Meet the GOODSTEAD Team

When you hire GOODSTEAD, these are the people who will be in your house. Here’s who they are and how we run a job.

Riley Hayes · Owner, GOODSTEAD

Most people call us with half an idea. That’s fine.

Usually it starts with something like “the kitchen just doesn’t work” or “we need another bedroom.” That’s enough to get going. I’ll come out, walk the house with you, look in the attic and the basement, and ask a lot of questions. Then I’ll tell you plainly what I think the job involves, what we won’t know until we open things up, and which decisions are yours to make. If it isn’t a job we’re the right fit for, I’ll tell you that too.

Riley Hayes

Our crew

Our own carpenters run every job.

GOODSTEAD carpenters are on your job most days. Electricians, plumbers and HVAC techs come in at set points, and one person is your contact for questions from start to finish. You’ll know who that is on day one.

We listen first
You tell us what’s wrong before we tell you what to build.
We explain it plainly
No jargon. If we don’t know something yet, we’ll say so.
Changes go in writing
Anything that changes the job gets written up and signed before we do it.
How we start a job↗

Who does what

Know who’s in charge of your job.

On the first visit we’ll tell you who runs your job day to day, who pulls the permits, whether you’ll need an architect or engineer, and how changes get written up and approved. All of that goes in the written scope.

Before you hire anyone

Check that the company is registered or licensed for your kind of work where you live, ask who will actually be on site, and ask how changes are handled. A good contractor will answer all three without hesitating.
